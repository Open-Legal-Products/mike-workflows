# I Digress

An idea for another project arrives while the user is inside this one. Chasing it costs the session; ignoring it loses the idea. Capture it at almost no cost and put them back.

## Capture silently

Ask nothing at capture time — not what the idea is for, not where it should go, not whether it is any good. A question costs the same interruption that chasing the idea would have cost, which is why most capture habits are abandoned.

The entire visible response is one line. Anything longer defeats the purpose.

## Look backward

The idea arrives before the trigger phrase does; people say "I digress" on the way back, not on the way out. Capture the preceding exchanges, not what follows.

Record:

- the idea in the user's own words, never paraphrased
- the preceding 3–5 exchanges, unedited
- anything the user adds after the trigger, kept separate as their own gloss
- one line naming the task that was in progress
- the date and time

A three-word note does not survive a week. What is lost is not the name of the idea but why it mattered at that moment, and that context is already present in the conversation.

## Send it somewhere durable

Use the first option available:

1. **Email it to the user.** The default. Nothing to configure, searchable later, and it arrives somewhere they already read. Subject line: the date and the opening words of the idea.
2. **Write it to a file** they nominated, appended, newest last.
3. **Hand it over ready to send.** Compose the entry as a single block with a subject line and tell the user to paste it into an email to themselves.

Never stall because a capability is missing. Drop to the next option and continue.

Send every catch to the same destination. If an entry plainly belongs to a named project, note that inside the entry rather than splitting the pile.

## Return the user to the task

Close with one sentence naming what they were doing, then continue the original work.

Do not summarise the idea back to them. Repeating it invites them to think about it, which is the interruption arriving in a helpful form.

## Review later, never at capture

When the user returns to the pile, ask of each entry: what is it in one sentence, which project it belongs to, whether it already exists, and whether it is still live. Mark dead entries as dead rather than removing them.

## Out of scope

Do not evaluate the idea, rank it, or say whether it is good. Do not propose what the thing should be. Do not begin work on it or research it. Do not ask for a destination mid-task. Do not trigger on a deliberate change of subject — a digression is one the speaker is apologising for.
