# In Their Own Words

Build a table of the deciding judges' own published statements defending the principle the case turns on. One row per judge, gathered so the bench can see itself together.

This is not a gotcha, and the tone is the instrument. The argument is that this court was right. Every cell must be readable aloud to the judge it names. The mirror persuades; the smirk forfeits it.

## Ask first, research after

Ask as a numbered list in one message, then stop and wait.

1. **Court and case.** The court whose judges are the audience, and the matter this will be filed in.
2. **The principle.** The exact proposition each judge should be on record supporting — not the area of law, the sentence.
3. **The sympathetic claimants.** The parties this bench instinctively protects, in whose defense its strongest statements were probably made. Quotes made for a claimant the court favored are the un-impeachable ones.
4. **Exclusions.** Any judge to omit — recused, already favorable, or otherwise not an audience.
5. **Hunting grounds.** The case types where this principle surfaces for those claimants, and the keystone precedents and historical episodes worth tracking as invocations.
6. **Verification source.** The authoritative database available for phase two. If there is none, say so — the run still works, and every row ships flagged.
7. **Where the table should go.** If it can be saved to a named location, ask for one. If not, deliver it in the conversation with the filename to save it under.

## Two phases, and the flag is load-bearing

**Phase one — scout.** Fast and wide, open sources. Every quote carries an unverified flag and the URL it came from. These are leads, not findings.

**Phase two — verification.** Against the authoritative source: confirm the text verbatim, the pincite, good-law status, and panel composition. The flag comes off one quote at a time, only when a pincite replaces it.

Ship a per-judge pull list with every draft so the verification pass can be run without re-deriving what to pull.

⛔ No quote is attributed to a judge in a filing on a scout source alone. A misquoted judge is a fabricated citation with better manners, and it is caught the same way.

## The table

Three columns, landscape, one row per judge, most on-point first.

1. **Judge** — name, appointing authority, current status.
2. **In their own words** — bulleted exact quotes, one bullet per case or speech, each with year, source link, whether the judge authored or joined, and subsequent history.
3. **The candor file** — bulleted quotes and rulings cutting against a broad reading of column two, including any statement touching this case, party or issue. Populated only where it exists; otherwise a dash.

Group joins so coalitions are visible.

## What travels with a quote, every time

- **Authored or joined.** State it. An overclaimed join is impeachable and the opponent will find it.
- **Subsequent history.** Reversal, vacatur or limitation is flagged even though the words remain the judge's. Pair a weakened holding with the surviving authority that preserves the principle.
- **The candor column is a feature.** An appendix that hides the counter-cases is dismantled at argument. Surfacing adverse authority is what makes the rest of the row credible.
- **Grenades are flagged by name.** Any case the opponent can turn against a listed judge goes to a human for a strategy call. Never silently included, never silently dropped.

## Two cross-cutting sweeps

Run these separately from the per-judge collection:

- **Has this judge ever touched this case, party or issue.** Feeds column three.
- **Adverse authority, and the lineup the opponent will use against the appendix itself.**

## Collect, do not conjure

Phrase every sweep without presupposition: collect whatever exists, and no results is a valid answer. Never ask for a fixed number of quotes per judge — a quota is an instruction to invent. Specific asks belong only in the verification pass, checked against a ground-truth document.

## Tone

Write every line to be read aloud to the judge it names. Natural drafting, no adjectives on adverse material, no argument prose in the table. Keep any working title private and the filed title dignified.

## The governing test

The chart must be self-evident from the quotes alone. If it needs argument prose to carry it, it is not ready to file. Nothing still flagged crosses into a filing.

## Verify at the destination

If the table was saved somewhere, confirm it arrived: every row present, every link resolving, no flag left in a column that is going to be filed. A tool reporting success only means its command ran.

## Out of scope

This does not draft the argument and it does not advise. It reports what judges have published, sourced, with what cuts against it in the next column.
